# Presentation Script — Art Direction First in a Hackathon

## Presentation purpose

Use this script to explain the repository to teammates, mentors, judges, or another AI builder. It is written for an approximately 7–10 minute walkthrough.

## Slide 1 — The problem

**On screen:** A generic AI-generated frontend beside a distinctive product interface.

**Say:**

“Most AI frontend tools are very good at producing a working page quickly. The problem is that they often start from familiar patterns: cards, pills, gradients, dashboards, and default typography. The result may be functional, but it does not feel like a product with a point of view. In a hackathon, that costs us time because we keep restyling the same generic result instead of making a strong decision early.”

## Slide 2 — The idea

**On screen:** Product truth → visual world → proof surfaces → full product.

**Say:**

“Art Direction First changes the order of operations. We begin with what the product means, who it serves, and what the user should feel. Then we define a visual world, a voice, a memorable metaphor, and a page-expression map. Only after that do we build. The process is reusable, but the visual identity is specific to each project.”

## Slide 3 — What the project owner provides

**On screen:** The project brief and asset/icon brief templates.

**Say:**

“The person using the system supplies the project-specific inputs: product truth, users, the core workflow, references, UI direction or morphism, fonts, icons, imagery, motion tolerance, technical boundaries, and quality bar. The skill does not force every product to look like LedgerLens. It gives the agent a disciplined way to make the right decisions for this product.”

## Slide 4 — References without copying

**On screen:** Three reference cards with ‘study for’ and ‘do not copy’ columns.

**Say:**

“We use three to seven references as research, not as templates. For every reference, we write what to study, where it applies, and what not to copy. Apptains might teach editorial confidence. Paymentor might teach product visualization. Another reference might teach information hierarchy. We synthesize principles into one original identity instead of making a collage.”

## Slide 5 — The visual concept gate

**On screen:** Three concept boards and the scorecard.

**Say:**

“Before we build every route, the agent creates three materially different visual concepts. They must show the entry surface and one real product workflow at desktop and mobile sizes. We score them on product truth, point of view, distinctiveness, typography, scale, whitespace, metaphor, accessibility, performance, and originality. A font or color variation is not a new concept.”

## Slide 6 — The two-surface proof

**On screen:** Landing page connected to the core product workflow.

**Say:**

“This is the most important time-saving step. We build only two proof surfaces first: the landing or entry experience and one complete real workflow. If they do not feel like the same product, we fix the direction before spending hours propagating it across the app. Speed comes from finding the wrong direction early.”

## Slide 7 — Using UI libraries correctly

**On screen:** A selected animation beside a rejected component-library collage.

**Say:**

“Libraries such as Animaster-style animated collections, Skiper UI, and Vengeance UI are optional resources. They do not define the brand. We select a component only when it has a product reason, fits the visual system, is accessible, performs well, and has a valid license. One meaningful interaction is better than ten unrelated effects.”

## Slide 8 — Building the rest of the product

**On screen:** Page-expression map.

**Say:**

“After the direction is locked, we propagate it by page responsibility. A landing page can be editorial and atmospheric. A queue should be fast and operational. A case workspace should be evidence-led. Settings should be precise. Audit should feel chronological and trustworthy. They share one visual world without becoming identical layouts.”

## Slide 9 — Quality and honesty

**On screen:** QA checklist and evidence matrix.

**Say:**

“Quality is not just a clean build. We test the live preview, responsive widths, keyboard navigation, focus, contrast, loading and error states, core success and failure paths, and any persistence or backend behavior the product claims. If a check was not possible, we mark it unverified. Honest limitations are better than a false completion claim.”

## Slide 10 — The hackathon outcome

**On screen:** A final product screenshot, `DESIGN.md`, and route evidence.

**Say:**

“The output is more than a polished landing page. We leave the hackathon with an ownable visual world, a project-specific `DESIGN.md`, one proven product workflow, reusable primitives, responsive screenshots, and a clear evidence report. The same repository can be handed to Antigravity, Claude Code, Gemini, Kiro, Cursor, or another agent because the process is tool-agnostic.”

## Closing statement

“Art Direction First does not ask us to make every project look the same. It asks us to stop letting the AI choose the visual identity by default. We provide the product truth, choose a point of view, prove it quickly, and then let the agent execute inside that world.”

## Demo handoff sentence

> “Here is the repository. Read `SKILL.md`, use `templates/agent-handoff.template.md`, fill in the project brief and asset brief, analyze the references, create three concepts, stop for screenshot review, then build the two-surface proof before touching the rest of the product.”
